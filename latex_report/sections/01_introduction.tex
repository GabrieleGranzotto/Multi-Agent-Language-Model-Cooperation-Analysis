\section{Introduction \& Research Question}
If a rational agent is faced with a strategic decision, it will typically choose the action that maximizes its expected utility given its beliefs about the other agents' actions. However, if the game is played by agents that are not perfectly rational and the trust starts to be a factor, the outcome may be different.

In this work, we explore the role of natural-language communication in strategic multi-agent interactions, specifically focusing on how pre-play communication can influence collective outcomes and defection incentives. We investigate whether allowing agents to engage in non-binding communication, often referred to as "cheap talk," can lead to emergent cooperation in scenarios where agents might otherwise defect.
